00 In one slide

The short version

Why

Procure is moving to self-service. CS currently reviews invoices on customers' behalf — that doesn't scale, and it keeps the customer dependent on us for their own finance process.

How

Make every gate in the pipeline legible. An invoice is never just "needs review" — the product says exactly which check failed, what it means in dollars, and what the options are.

What

Four screens — Uploads, Needs your input, Processed, and the invoice detail — plus the policy controls (matching mode, tolerances) that used to live in a config file only CS knew about.

Scope: the Invoices section only. Upstream ordering/GRN and downstream accounting sync behaviour are unchanged — this is about who can resolve an exception, and whether the UI gives them enough to do it.

01 Why

Self-service is the direction
for all of Procure.

This revamp is one workstream in that wider push. Invoices is the highest-touch surface we have left — it's where CS involvement is heaviest, so it's where self-service pays back fastest.

Today: CS in the loop

Our staff review flagged invoices, work out what's wrong, and tell the customer what to do. Every exception costs us human time and costs the customer a wait.

Tomorrow: customer in control

The person who placed the order resolves the exception themselves — they already know whether that extra case of tomatoes was agreed by phone. We just have to show them the discrepancy.

What has to change

Signposting. Every state needs a plain-language reason, a dollar impact where one exists, and an obvious next action — because there's no longer a human to translate.

02 Why

What today's experience
doesn't tell the user.

Three gaps come up repeatedly, and each one is a reason a customer has to ask us instead of acting.

Today
  • A flagged invoice says "needs review" — with no indication of which check failed or by how much.
  • Matching is a single pass/fail. If it fails, you can't see how far it got or what's still outstanding.
  • Matching policy and tolerances live in a config file — only CS knows they exist, let alone what they're set to.
  • No way to see what the source document actually said next to what we extracted from it.
Proposed
  • Every exception carries a named reason — Price, Quantity, Extra items, Unmapped item — and its dollar impact.
  • An 11-point completeness checklist per invoice: what passed, what failed, what's still waiting on someone else.
  • Matching mode and tolerances are controls in the product, shown wherever they affect what you're looking at.
  • The source document sits beside the extracted data on the detail screen, so a mismatch is checkable in one glance.
03 Why

Design principles
for a self-service queue.

Name the blocker, not the state

"Awaiting GRN (3 of 6 lines)" beats "Pending". The user should never have to ask what the status means.

Quantify the impact

An exception isn't real to a customer until it has a number: "S$170 overbilled" is a decision; "price variance" is a shrug.

One obvious next action

Each state offers the action that state actually needs — raise a credit note, accept short-shipped, link a PO — not a generic Save.

Policy belongs in the product

If a setting changes what auto-posts, the user who lives with the consequences should be able to see and change it.

Show the evidence

Every claim the system makes should be checkable against the source document without leaving the screen.

Safe to act

Reasons captured on destructive actions, an audit trail on every edit, and no silent auto-posting outside the stated tolerance.

04 How

The pipeline, end to end.

Every invoice sits in exactly one stage at a time and moves forward as its data completes. Nothing sits in two queues at once.

01

Capture

Photo, PDF, email attachment or PEPPOL — all four land in the same queue and get the same checks.

02

Identify

Read the document, identify the supplier, capture invoice number and date, rule out a duplicate.

03

Match

Attach a PO, compare the item set, then price and quantity against the PO and the goods receipt.

04

Decide

Clean matches auto-clear. Anything outside tolerance routes to a human with a named reason.

05

Post & export

Approved invoices post, then sync to the accounting system and become read-only.

Stages 01–03 are the system's work and live in Uploads. Stage 04 is the human's work and lives in Needs your input. Stage 05 lives in Processed.

06 How

Six statuses, three queues.

The status is derived from the invoice's own data every time something changes — it's never set by hand and never goes stale.

Pending

In flight — being identified, matched, or waiting on a goods receipt. No human action possible yet, or a quick data fix is offered inline.

Uploads

A blocking exception that needs a decision: price above PO, an unmapped item, a total that won't reconcile.

Needs your input

07 How

The policy controls
we're bringing into the open.

These decide what "clean" means. They're org-wide settings, not per-invoice toggles — and changing one re-evaluates every invoice currently waiting, immediately.

Matching mode

3-way (default) checks price and quantity against what was actually received. 2-way drops the goods-receipt leg for suppliers who never raise one — services, subscriptions. No matching drops both legs — no PO required, price and quantity never checked — for teams that would rather process as-is than block on matching at all. Item identity is the one check that survives every tier: an unmapped item still needs a person, matching on or off. Each step down states plainly what control it costs.

Tolerances

Price and quantity variance thresholds — the line between auto-post and human review. Defaults of ±2% price and ±1% quantity, editable from the queue where they apply.

Date range

Scopes the summary metrics and every queue to a period, so "awaiting review" is a number for a month you can act on rather than an all-time total.

Open for discussion: tolerances are currently org-wide. A per-supplier override is the obvious next ask — a supplier with volatile produce pricing shouldn't force a loose tolerance on everyone else.

08 What · Screen 1 of 4

Uploads

Everything still in flight. The job of this screen is to answer one question per row: what is this waiting on, and can I do something about it?

Invoices → Uploads
1

"What's blocking" column

Plain language per row — "Awaiting remaining GRN (3/6)", "No PO linked", "Possible duplicate of INV-00881" — instead of a status word.

Removes the most common reason to contact CS.
2

Completeness checklist

Click the blocker to see all 11 checkpoints with pass / fail / waiting / not-applicable, so the user can see how far the invoice got and what's left.

Replaces a single opaque pass/fail.
3

Source badges and page count

Email, Web, Mobile or PEPPOL shown per row, with a page count for multi-page captures, and an unread marker for anything nobody has opened yet.

Makes triage possible without opening rows.
4

Inline fixes

Link a PO by type-ahead, confirm or dismiss a duplicate, request a clearer re-upload — the fixes happen here, not in a separate flow.

Most Uploads blockers are data entry, not judgement.
5

Guard-railed delete

Only available before approval, requires a reason, warns if a PO is already attached, and stays recoverable for audit.

Self-service needs safe destructive actions.
09 What · Screen 2 of 4

Needs your input

The only queue that genuinely requires a person. Every row states the exception, the dollars involved, and what the supplier appears to have done.

Invoices → Needs your input
1

Named exception per row

A reason tag — Price, Quantity, Extra items, Items missing, Unmapped item, Total mismatch — plus a sentence of evidence: "GRN confirms 8 received, invoice bills for 12 — S$106 overbilled".

The customer can act without interpreting anything.
2

Filter by reason

Work one exception type at a time — every price variance in one pass — rather than context-switching down a mixed list.

Matches how finance teams actually batch work.
3

Bulk resolve — as an explicit override

Not "approve everything under tolerance": nothing in this queue is under tolerance, by definition — that's why it's here. So the bulk action is named for what it actually does: "Approve 2 price variances anyway", "Raise 3 credit notes", "Accept 2 as short-shipped".

Describes the decision honestly instead of implying it's routine.
4

One reason at a time

The button only enables when the selection shares a single reason, because the right resolution differs per reason — a credit note for over-billing, accepting a short shipment, an override on price. Mixed selections say so and point you at the reason filter.

Stops one click applying the wrong remedy to half the batch.
5

Composition problems can't be bulk-resolved

Unmapped items, extra items and item sets that don't match the PO need a per-item judgement or a data fix, so the button stays disabled and says why. If the fix is about money you can decide for a batch; if it's about data, open the invoice.

Keeps bulk actions to decisions that are genuinely batchable.
6

A confirmation with real numbers

States the actual count, the actual total, the reason being overridden and the dollar variance being waved through — then records the resolution against each invoice, visible afterwards in Processed under "How it posted".

Today's bulk-tolerance logic asks for no confirmation at all.
7

Tolerance shown as the cause, not the action

The bulk bar reads "Fell outside ±2% price · ±1% qty", with a Change link — framing the tolerance as the reason these invoices are in front of you.

Policy surfaced in context, not buried in settings.
10 What · Screen 3 of 4

Processed

Everything resolved — split by whether it still needs a click from you or is already out the door.

Invoices → Processed
1

Action needed vs Exported

A two-state filter so "done" doesn't hide invoices that are approved but not yet synced to the accounting system.

The gap between approved and exported is where money gets stuck.
2

"How it posted"

Each row records whether it auto-cleared or was resolved by a person, and which exception it carried — the audit view of the queue.

Lets a manager sanity-check what the agent is clearing.
3

Sync state per invoice

Export to the accounting system from the row, with synced invoices going read-only and keeping their trail.

One less system to check.
11 What · Screen 4 of 4

Invoice detail

The focused review surface. Queue-level chrome — summary metrics, tabs, global actions — is hidden here, because the only question on this screen is what to do about this invoice.

Invoices → Invoice detail
1

Status header and contextual action bar

The blocker in plain language, and only the actions that state allows — Post invoice, Raise credit note, Accept as short-shipped, Request re-upload, Reject.

No generic toolbar the user has to decode.
2

Three-part hierarchy

Invoice details → match summary → line items. The match bar is visually its own band so it reads as something the system is telling you, not a field you filled in.

Separates evidence from input.
3

All three documents on one line

Every line carries the invoiced qty and price next to the GRN qty received and the PO price agreed, plus its own verdict — "6.2% vs PO", "Qty +4 vs GRN", "Not on PO-5530", "Awaiting GRN". Under 2-way matching the GRN column disappears.

The variance is arithmetic the reviewer can see, not a claim they have to trust.
4

Goods receipt named on the invoice

The GRN reference sits beside the order number — the two documents being matched against — with how many lines have actually been received against it.

"Awaiting GRN" stops being a mystery: you can see which receipt, and how much of it landed.
5

One item, one row — even split across POs

When a consolidated invoice bills the same item against two POs, it's one row, not two: quantities and amount summed, a breakdown underneath ("18 via PO-5512 · 5 via PO-5528"), and each PO's own verdict stacked under Match rather than one silently standing in for both. A new PO qty column shows what was actually ordered next to what was received and invoiced. Adding a second PO ("+ Add another PO") or removing one is done right in the Order number field — no separate screen.

Two rows for one item reads as a data error before it reads as a deliberate split.
6

Source document alongside

A rendering of the captured document — invoice and delivery dates, PO reference, bill-to/deliver-to, line items, GST and total — next to the extracted data.

Verify an extraction without opening the original file.
7

Edit history and pager

Every line edit is attributed and timestamped; arrow keys page through the filtered queue you arrived from.

Review a batch without bouncing back to the list.
12 What

Supporting surfaces.

Smaller flows that the four screens lean on.

Upload

One modal for every source, sized for a whole batch landing at once. Every dropped file gets a readability check inline; the default is one invoice per file, with an explicit "combine into one invoice" step for the files that are actually pages of the same document.

Matching settings

3-way vs 2-way, with the control trade-off stated in the modal rather than assumed knowledge. Saving re-evaluates the queue immediately.

Tolerance settings

Price and quantity thresholds with plain-language explanations of what each one lets through, reachable from the queue it governs.

Delete upload

Reason required, downstream warning if a PO is linked, retained for audit and recoverable by a controller.

13 Scope

What this revamp is not.

Naming these up front so review stays on the part we're actually proposing.

Not a change to the matching engine. Tolerances, duplicate rules and extraction confidence thresholds keep their current definitions — we're surfacing them, not re-deriving them.
Not a change to ordering or goods receipt. POs and GRNs are consumed as-is; anything upstream is a separate workstream.
Not an accounting integration change. Export behaviour and the supported targets are unchanged.
Not removing CS entirely. The goal is that CS handles genuine escalations, not routine variance review.
Not a mobile app. Capture on mobile already works, and the Invoices review flow now reflows down to phone width (a hamburger drawer replaces the sidebar; the two-column detail screen stacks) — but this is a responsive web pass on the existing screens, not a native app or a mobile-specific redesign.
Not wired to a cloud OCR service. The per-file "readable" check on upload is a file-size heuristic only (small non-PDF photos are flagged), not a real quality read — deliberately, so we're not taking on a per-page vendor cost and API dependency at prototype stage. Swapping in a real OCR-based check later doesn't change the UX, only what runs behind it.
14 To decide

Open questions.

Where I'd most like a view before this goes further.

Should tolerances be scoped per supplier?

Org-wide is simple but blunt. Produce suppliers move on price far more than dry goods — one global threshold will either be too loose or too noisy.

Who is allowed to change matching policy?

2-way materially lowers control; No matching drops both legs at once. Should either be finance-only, or is outlet-level fine with an audit note — and should No matching need a higher bar than 2-way to turn on?

Do we need an undo window on auto-posting?

The agent spec has one. This revamp shows the audit trail but doesn't expose an undo — is that a gap for launch or a fast-follow?

What's the escalation path to CS?

If we're pulling CS out of routine review, the product needs an explicit "get help on this invoice" route, or people will just email anyway.

How do we measure success?

Proposed: exceptions resolved without a CS touch, median time from upload to export, and auto-clear rate — but worth agreeing targets before build.
